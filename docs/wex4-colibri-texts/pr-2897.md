## 1. What this adds

- **`make check` green again on the WEX stack.** `tests/test_makefile_deps.py` failed from #2883 onward: `tests/test_wex_nr3_gpu` is built by nvcc (no `.d`), now listed in `AUTODEP_OWN_FLAGS` (6 failures: default build, VK / CUDA / HIP / XDNA flavours, hand-listed headers). Once `tests/test_wex_fast_decode` is built a 7th appears ("no source found in its rule": #2888 named its source through a variable); its three rules now name the `.c` and take headers from `-MMD`. The loader test's stand-in (`tests/fake_wex_cuda.so` / `.dll`) is in `.gitignore` and `tools/clean.py`.
- **`coli_wex_decode_record_to(entry, record, scales[3], weights[3])`** (`wex_decode.c`): a WNR2/WNR3 record held in a buffer of its stored size decoded straight into the caller's six buffers. Same decoders, checks and bytes as `coli_wex_decode_record`; no page of the decoded size, no decoder scratch, no copy. The CUDA helper, where it takes the record, still decodes through its own slab (then the CPU on any status but 0, as before).
  - DeepSeek V4.1 `v41_wex_fetch_nr3`: the record into a pooled buffer of its stored size, decoded into the slot's six windows. A refused record is read again from the shards over whatever the decode left (unchanged path).
  - Qwen3.8 `q38_x4_read_nr3`: decoded into the expert buffer. (The published Qwen3.8 container holds RAW records, so this matters for WNR3 Qwen3.8 containers only.)
- **The fast decoder's stream count measured at run time** (`COLI_WEX_FAST_LANES`): the first 16 WNR3 records try 3, 4, 6, 8 streams in turn, timed on the thread's CPU clock (`CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID`; `QueryThreadCycleTime` on Windows), so 8 readers on 4 cores do not skew it; a refused record hands its turn to the next. The best time per byte is used from then on, one `[wex]` line says which. `COLI_WEX_FAST_LANES=1|2|3|4|6|8` fixes it. Speed only: same bytes, same verdicts for every count (#2888's tests).

## 2. Why

- Memory: V4.1 REAP-192 records are 18.8 MB decoded, ~16.1 MB stored. A reader held a page of the decoded size plus the decoder's scratch of the same size: 37.6 MB; now 16.1 MB. With `V41_READ_DEPTH` 8: ~301 MB -> ~129 MB of transient buffers. Peak RSS above the preload, 1 reader: 36 -> 16 MB (measured).
- Speed: the best stream count depends on the core (Wex-WRN2's RESULTS.md: 4 on its x86 desktops, 6 on a Neoverse-N2, 8 on Apple M1 VMs). 4 stays the default before the measurement ends and on CPUs where it is best.

## 3. Measured (this sandbox: Intel Xeon @ 2.1 GHz, 4 vCPU, AVX2/AVX-512, Ubuntu 24.04, gcc 13.3)

Real records: 16 WNR3 records of the published `Kaira666/Kaira-WEX4-Experts` `v41-reap/v4_flash_experts.nr3.wex` (header, index, WNM2 blob and the records fetched by HTTP range into a sparse file; spread over the container as `test_wex_fast_decode --bench` picks them). Decoded from memory (disk not timed), the V4.1 reader path: each record placed in six windows; 64 records a pass, best of 3 passes, median of 5 runs, alternating builds.

| readers | #2889 (page + `coli_wex_decode_record` + 6 memcpy, 4 streams) | direct, 4 streams fixed | direct, measured (picked 6 in 10 of 10 runs) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 31.3 ms/record | 29.0 (-7%) | **25.6 (-18%)** |
| 4 | 31.7 | 29.0 (-9%) | **25.8 (-19%)** |

An earlier, noisier session (the full `make test-c` running in parallel part of the time; 12 records measured) gave 33.8 / 31.5 / 28.4 (8 readers) and 34.0 / 32.1 / 29.1 (4 readers): the same order.

One thread, per stream count (best of 3, ms/record): 3: 140.1, 4: 118.3, 6: 103.6, 8: 134.7.

Not measured here: a whole-engine run (no V4.1 checkpoint in the sandbox), Windows, ARM. On a CPU where 4 is best the measurement picks 4 (with wall-clock timing it was fooled by 8 readers on 4 cores and picked 3; the thread CPU clock fixed that: 6 in every run since), so no loss there beyond the 16 measured records.
